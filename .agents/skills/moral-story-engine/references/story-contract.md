# Story contract

KindPath compiles a parent's lesson into a child-safe adventure in six typed stages:

1. Moral policy converts the lesson into behavior, motive, consequences, repair, forbidden treatments, and one policy decision.
2. Premise generation produces exactly three story-first candidates, then a separate ranker evaluates all three. The moral controls the decision axis, not the external plot.
3. Hierarchical outlining locks setup, both mini-arcs, bridges, shared finale, and reflection before typed beats and states are compiled.
4. Story graph compilation produces a shared setup, one binary choice, two complete mini-arcs, branch end states, convergence bridges, and a shared finale.
5. An independent judge scores the outline and graph without writing either one.
6. Shot compilation converts the validated graph into eight bounded generation segments.

A premise passes the storyness contract only when it has a non-moral external goal, a meaningful relationship, escalation before the choice, setup/payoff, a constructive action requiring effort, a tempting alternative with an understandable motive, and a proportionate natural consequence.

The typed graph must map every setup-phase beat to a real later payoff or resolution on both paths. Beat IDs are globally unique, and state-fact references are valid only when their character, prop, relationship pair, or promise ID is declared by canon and typed states.

The writer, editor, and judge are separate roles. A stage may retry after deterministic or semantic feedback, but invalid artifacts never flow downstream.

Parent approval occurs after the package passes validation and before any video quota is used. Sensitive topics require an explicit acknowledgement, and the approved story copy persists that decision.
